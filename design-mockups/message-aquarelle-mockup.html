Elowen
the meadow gate · first light

She straightens from the tall grass, an armful of wet-stemmed blooms held to her chest, dew still bright on the petals. The whole field is silver with it, and the light comes soft and low over the hedgerows.

"You're up before the bees." A slow, pleased smile. "Good. The best of them open first — and they don't wait for the late risers."

Here. This one's for you. Mind the stem, it bleeds a little green if you crush it.
🧠 reasoning
Keep her warm and unhurried — she measures the world in seasons, not minutes. Let the flowers do the flirting she won't quite voice.
You
knee-deep in dew

I wade the last few steps out of the long grass, boots dark to the ankle, and stop just short of her.

"The bees can keep their hours." I take the flower she offers, careful of the green. "I came for the company, not the pollen. Show me which ones open next."
