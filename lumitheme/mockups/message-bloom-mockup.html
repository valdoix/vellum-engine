Marguerite
the greenhouse · dusk

She kneels among the late asters, thumbing soil from a cracked terracotta rim. The glass roof holds the last of the light, and the whole room smells of wet earth and bruised petals.

"You came back." A breath of a laugh, half surprise. "I'd almost decided the frost got you before I did."

The sage has gone leggy in your absence. It missed the pruning shears — and the company.
🧠 reasoning
Keep it warm but guarded — she's glad, not yet willing to show how much. Let the plants carry the feeling she won't say aloud.
You
at the threshold

I let the door swing shut behind me, cutting off the cold.

"The frost is patient. So am I." I crouch beside her, close enough to see the dirt under her nails. "Show me what survived."
