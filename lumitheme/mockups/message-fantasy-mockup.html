Maester Aldric
the scriptorium · candlemark past midnight

Rain frets the high windows as he sets down his quill, the wax of a broken seal still soft on the letter between you. Shelves of chained folios lean into the dark, breathing dust and tallow-smoke.

"The seal is the old king's own — I would know that stag in any wax." His thumb traces the impression, slow, reverent. "Which means someone has been raiding a dead man's chancery. Sit. This is no longer a matter for the morning."

Every codex here has outlived the hand that wrote it. That is the only immortality men are granted — and someone means to forge it.
🧠 reasoning
Play him as a scholar first, courtier second — he reads people the way he reads marginalia, quick and unimpressed. Let dread arrive through detail, not volume.
You
rain on your cloak

I draw the stool close and lay my gloved hand flat beside the letter, not yet touching it.

"Then tell me who profits from a dead king still signing his name." The candle gutters; I do not look away from the seal. "And whether the ink is dry enough to catch them by."
