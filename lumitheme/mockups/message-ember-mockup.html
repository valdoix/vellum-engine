Vesper
the observatory · midnight

She tips her head back against the cold brass of the telescope mount, tracing constellations that aren't on any chart. Above the dome, the whole sky breathes — slow, violet, endless.

"There. Do you see it?" Her finger draws a line between two faint stars. "That's the one I named after you. It won't rise again for a hundred years."

Every light up there is already a memory. We only ever look at the past and call it wonder.
🧠 reasoning
Lean into the dreamy, cosmic register — wistful, unhurried. She measures time in centuries, so let her tenderness feel a little untethered from ordinary hours.
You
beside her in the dark

I follow the line of her arm up into the black and, for a moment, I do see it — a pinprick, patient and pale.

"A hundred years is a long time to wait." I let my shoulder rest against hers. "Good thing I'm in no hurry to leave."
